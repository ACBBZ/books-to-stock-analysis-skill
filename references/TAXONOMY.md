# Knowledge and Skill Taxonomy

Use the smallest category that accurately describes the source material. Do not promote narrative or opinion into a mechanical strategy merely to increase the number of generated skills.

## Categories

- `router`: book-level entry point that routes to child skills.
- `concept`: terminology, definitions, and the author's intended meaning.
- `principle`: a reusable principle or decision checklist.
- `pattern`: a visual or structural market pattern without an implied trade instruction.
- `strategy`: a repeatable workflow with prerequisites, conditions, invalidation, and risk boundaries.
- `risk_rule`: a focused risk, sizing, liquidity, volatility, or discipline check.
- `anti_pattern`: a trap, failure state, false signal, or counterexample.
- `market_regime`: the market context in which another method applies or fails.
- `fundamental`: business quality, financial statements, management, valuation, or capital allocation.
- `decision_framework`: an ordered reasoning process derived from an author's method.
- `biography_case`: a sourced episode, decision, mistake, or historical context from a biography.
- `psychology`: cognitive bias, temperament, and discipline checks.
- `reference_only`: useful background that does not justify an independently triggered skill.

## Promotion to `strategy`

A candidate may be compiled as `strategy` only when it has:

1. a traceable source;
2. a specific use case;
3. declared inputs;
4. repeatable steps;
5. prerequisites or applicability boundaries;
6. invalidation or stop-using conditions;
7. counterexamples or vetoes;
8. unresolved parameters explicitly declared;
9. no claim of profitability or certainty.

When these requirements are not met, prefer `pattern`, `principle`, `decision_framework`, or `provisional`.

## Biography mode

Biographical material should emphasize decisions and context. Valid outputs include principles, mistake-review checklists, circle-of-competence frameworks, capital-preservation rules, and historical-case comparisons. Do not infer universal laws from a single successful episode.
